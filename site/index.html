How it works

  1. National environment. The mean of five generic-ballot polling averages (two-party), blended with an estimated fundamentals model: Gallup approval, the previous House result and the real change in gas prices, with inflation, income and unemployment tested and found weak.
  2. Race priors. Each race starts at 2×PVI + national environment + incumbency (+2.5 House, +2 Senate). PVI is on the 2026 district lines, so it reflects the mid-decade redistricting in 11 states.
  3. Poll averages. Recency-weighted (21-day half-life), scaled by √sample size, likely voters preferred, prolific pollsters down-weighted, and party-sponsored polls shifted 2 pts (Senate) or 4 pts (House) against the sponsor.
  4. Blend. Poll average and prior are combined by precision.
  5. Simulation. 40,000 draws with a shared national polling error, Census-region shocks, a random slope on state lean, and race-specific errors, all fat-tailed. Control: 218 House seats; 51 Senate seats (VP Vance breaks ties).
  6. Two headline versions. Face value takes the polls as they are. Adjusted shifts the polls (not the fundamentals) by the historical tendency of final polls to overstate Democrats. See Polling bias.

Judgment calls and caveats
  • Fundamentals extrapolate. 2026 approval (Gallup-equivalent ~35.6%) is below every past midterm, and the 29% real rise in gas prices is above anything in the 1962–2022 data.
  • House model. The district error (SD 6) and incumbency (+2.5) are assumptions and weren't backtested; the seat total matches Silver Bulletin's.
  • Michigan. Haley Stevens endorsed Mike Rogers on Nov 1, after most polls were taken. Not in the model.
  • Alaska uses ranked-choice voting and has several "Dan Sullivan" candidates on the ballot; modeled as a head-to-head. Montana is a three-way race, modeled from polls only.
  • Independents (Osborn NE, Achilles ID, Bengs SD) are counted separately from either party. CA-6 (Kevin Kiley, independent) is counted for neither party.
  • District polls are sparse and often party-sponsored; they're shrunk toward the prior.

Polling bias

How far the final generic-ballot average missed the national House vote, two-party, 1998–2022. Positive = Democrats beat their polls.

Re-estimating the bias for 2026 (decay weights and covariates)

Elections are weighted by recency (half-lives of 6–16 years), midterms 2–3× presidential years, and covariates are tested: party in the White House, House majority, second term, and a president-specific effect. Specifications are compared by leave-one-election-out error.

Technical appendix

A. Fundamentals model (estimated)

Outcome: the two-party House vote margin of the president's party, 1962–2022. Predictors are measured before the election: Gallup approval (polls ending July 15–October 31), the president's party's House margin two years earlier, CPI inflation and the real change in gasoline prices (September over September), real disposable income per capita growth, and the change and level of unemployment. Every subset of up to four predictors is fit by OLS and scored by leave-one-election-out RMSE; the 2026 estimate averages the top five, weighted by 1/RMSE².

B. Error model

For race r in simulation s: margin = μr + σnat·zs + uregion(r),s + bs·leanr + εr,s. The national shock z is t(5), shared by every House and Senate race; region shocks u (four Census regions) and the lean slope b are normal; ε is t(5) with variance sdrace² − σregion² − σb²·lean² (floored at a quarter of the total), so each race's own probability stays calibrated and only the joint distribution changes.

C. Estimators at a glance

  • Poll weight = ½age/half-life × √(min(n, 3000)/600) × population weight (LV 1.0, RV 0.8, adults 0.5) ÷ √(polls by that pollster). Half-life 21 days for races, 14 for the generic ballot. Two-party margins throughout.
  • Poll-average SD (Senate) = √(7.4² + max(spread, 4)²/neff), neff = (Σw)²/Σw². Blend: μ = (μpoll/σpoll² + μprior/σprior²)/(1/σpoll² + 1/σprior²), σprior = 10 (Senate), 6 (House).
  • National SDs: RMSE of the final generic-ballot miss (face value); predictive SD of the decay-weighted bias estimate (adjusted). The Senate national SD is the Senate per-cycle miss RMSE, scaled the same way.
  • Calibration data: 538's raw_polls.csv (final 21 days, 1998–2022), averaged within race before comparison with results.
  • Monte Carlo error: 40,000 draws, so a probability near 50% has a standard error of about 0.25 points.
