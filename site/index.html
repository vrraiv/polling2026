How the model works

  1. National environment. Polls-only: the mean of the generic-ballot aggregators, converted to a two-party margin. Polls + fundamentals: that number blended, by inverse variance, with a fundamentals estimate from presidential approval and real disposable income growth. The polls get about 70% of the weight.
  2. Race priors. Each race starts at 2×PVI + national environment + incumbency (+2.5 points for House incumbents, +2 for Senate). PVI is Cook-style, on the 2026 district lines, so it already reflects mid-decade redistricting in TX, CA, MO, NC, UT, OH, VA, FL, TN, LA and AL.
  3. Poll averages. Recency-weighted (21-day half-life), scaled by √sample size, likely voters preferred over registered voters and adults. Pollsters with many polls are down-weighted, and partisan-sponsored polls are shifted against their sponsor (2 pts for Senate, 4 pts for House district polls).
  4. Blend. Poll average and prior are combined by precision. The poll-average error is set from history (race-level SD about 6 pts); the Senate prior's error is assumed at 10 pts and the House district error at 6 pts.
  5. Simulation. 40,000 draws. Each draw shares one national polling error across every race (generic-ballot RMSE 3.6 pts; Senate 3.1 pts), plus independent race errors. All errors are fat-tailed (t-distribution, 5 df).
  6. Control. House: 218 seats; same-party and uncontested seats are fixed; CA-6 (Kevin Kiley, independent) is counted for neither party. Senate: Democrats need 51 seats (VP Vance breaks ties); 34 Dem-caucus and 31 Republican seats are not up. Independents (Osborn, NE; Achilles, ID) are reported separately.

Judgment calls & caveats

  • Fundamentals coefficients are assumed, not estimated. Two midterms can't identify them. President's-party House margin = −4 + 0.6×(approval − 45) + 0.5×(real disposable income growth − 1.5).
  • Polling bias. Since 1998, final generic-ballot averages have overstated Democrats by 2.7 pts on average (they were accurate in 2018). The scenario button shifts every race by that amount.
  • Michigan. Haley Stevens endorsed Mike Rogers on Nov 1, after most polls were taken. This isn't in the model.
  • Alaska uses ranked-choice voting and has several "Dan Sullivan" candidates on the ballot. It's modeled as a head-to-head. Montana is a genuine three-way race, modeled from polls only (best non-Republican vs. Republican).
  • District polls are sparse, and many are sponsored by parties or PACs (48 of 132 since July are Democratic-sponsored). They are shrunk toward the prior.

Backtest: 2018 & 2022

The same poll-averaging and uncertainty method, run on archived 538 polls up to five days before each election and scored against actual results. Polls-only, without the PVI prior. The error scales were fitted on data that include these cycles, so this is an in-sample check.
